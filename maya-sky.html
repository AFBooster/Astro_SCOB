The Maya were the great timekeepers of the ancient world. From the rainforests of Mesoamerica they watched the sky with astonishing care — above all the Sun, the Moon and, more than any other people, the planet Venus — and wove what they saw into interlocking calendars of breathtaking precision.

Their sky begins with creation. In the Popol Vuh, the gods set three hearthstones and lit the first fire — the stars of Orion, with the glowing Orion Nebula as its smoke; and the cosmic Turtle, from whose shell the Maize God was reborn, is Orion's belt.

Along the sky road (the ecliptic) march the calendar animals: Tzab, the rattlesnake's rattle (the Pleiades); the Scorpion; the Peccaries (Gemini). And at the still pivot of the turning sky sits Xaman Ek', the North Star, guide of merchants.

🐉🪷 The same stars, a new world's names. Tzab is China's 昴 and India's Kṛttikā; the Turtle is 參 / Mṛgaśīrṣa. Each figure below shows its matching Chinese mansion and Indian nakshatra.

Chak Ek' — the Venus star — & the Maya calendars

No people tracked Venus like the Maya. Over eight years the planet's five appearances as morning-and-evening star trace a five-pointed star across the sky — the rhythm at the heart of the Dresden Codex.

The 584-day star. Venus returns to the same place in the sky-and-Sun cycle every 583.92 days — its synodic period. Five of these cycles take almost exactly eight years (5 × 584 ≈ 8 × 365), so Venus draws a near-perfect five-pointed star. The Maya knew this to within hours across centuries.

Kukulkan. Venus as morning star was the feathered serpent returning; its heliacal rising after conjunction was watched with dread and used to time war and ritual.

The calendars. The 260-day sacred Tzolk'in and the 365-day Haab' mesh like gears into the 52-year Calendar Round — and the vast Long Count counted the days from creation itself.

Maya sky-lore survives in the codices (Dresden, Paris), the Popol Vuh, carved monuments and living Maya communities; securely mapped figures are few, so this is a faithful, representative selection.
