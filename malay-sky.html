The Malay world is a world of sea and rice-field, and its sky was read for two things above all: finding your way across the archipelago, and knowing when to plant. So the Nusantara sky is not a grid of mansions but a small set of vivid, useful star-pictures — bintang — remembered in stories and passed down by word of mouth.

A navigator learned that Bintang Pari, the stingray (the Southern Cross), points the way south; that Bintang Biduk, the boat (the Big Dipper), rides low in the north; and that Bintang Belantik (Orion) rises due east. A farmer watched Bintang Tujuh (the Pleiades) to time the rice.

Layered on top of this indigenous lore came the buruj — the twelve zodiac constellations, brought through Islamic almanac scholarship (ilmu falak) with their Arabic names. In the traditional Malay taqwim they mark the seasons and help find the qibla.

Two more sights every Malay child knew: Bima Sakti, the glowing river of the Milky Way, named for the hero Bhima; and Bintang Timur / Bintang Kejora — bright Venus, the "eastern star" that heralds the dawn.

The 12 Buruj — the zodiac — بروج

The Arabic-Malay zodiac of the taqwim almanac: twelve signs of 30° along the Sun's yearly path, each with its Arabic name. Tap one to read it.

🌙 Bintang & buruj. The indigenous bintang are what you actually see and steer by; the buruj are the scholarly zodiac used to reckon the calendar and the qibla. They meet in the sky — e.g. Bintang Kala Jengking (the scorpion) is the buruj Al-'Aqrab.

Star-pictures are drawn from their brighter member stars to about magnitude 5 (Jurong's practical city-sky limit) at Hipparcos J2000 positions. Identifications follow folk-astronomy studies of the Malay Peninsula and the wider Nusantara (Malay, Javanese & Bugis traditions); names vary by region and are given in Rumi with the traditional Jawi script (spellings follow common orthography and may vary). The 12 buruj follow the Arabic zodiac used in the Malay taqwim. The Malay sky is a living oral tradition, not a fixed catalogue — this is a representative selection, not an exhaustive list.
