🌙 The Arabic Sky

Science Centre Observatory, Jurong · the 28 lunar mansions & the stars we still name in Arabic
← Dashboard

Long before telescopes, the desert Arabs measured the year by the Moon. Each night it moves about 13° eastward against the stars, and in ~27 nights it circles the whole sky — passing through 28 way-stations, the منازل القمر manāzil al-qamar, "the mansions of the Moon".

Each mansion is a small star-group the Moon "lodges" in for a night. Together they form a calendar and a weather-almanac: the anwāʾ system tied the dawn rising or setting of each mansion to a season of rain, heat, wind or harvest — a farmer's and herder's sky, read without a single instrument.

This is the third of the great lunar-mansion systems, alongside the Chinese 28 宿 (xiù) and the Indian 27 nakshatras — and they share most of their marker stars, because they all watch the same Moon on the same path. But the Arabic sky left a mark the others didn't: when this astronomy passed into medieval Europe, its star names came with it. Aldebaran, Altair, Deneb, Rigel, Betelgeuse, Fomalhaut, Vega, Algol — the everyday names of the brightest stars are Arabic, worn smooth by a thousand years of use.

The framework — 28 mansions inside 12 signs — al-burūj

The Moon keeps 28 stations; the Sun keeps 12 signs. Arabic astronomy carried these twelve بروج (burūj), sidereally reckoned, into medieval Europe — the zodiac we still use. The mansions are the finer ruler laid over the same circle.
